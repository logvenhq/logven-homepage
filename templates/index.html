Thesis

Everyone built digital shadows of their organisation. We built the twin that runs it.

The idea

A company is state plus processes. State is its data. Processes are the functions that act on that data. Logven makes both explicit in one versioned system: GitHub for the state of the company.

The twin is built from executed work. It is not drawn or maintained by hand. Agents do the work, stable work is compiled into code, and inputs become state.

Emails, documents, communications and numbers are simple data points, and together they are the whole company state. Because the model sits at a higher level of abstraction than any single task, improving it improves the work itself.

The problem

Specialised technical teams spend their time on manual work around data. They extract values from PDFs, update spreadsheets and databases, consolidate and analyse, write reports, process responses and resolve anomalies.

Leaders have no live view of how this work runs, so they cannot standardise expertise or optimise a department. Existing digital twins of an organisation mirror the company after the fact and do not change the work.

How it works

Agents are the prototype. Software is the product. Teams work with agent workers much like they would with Claude. Logven watches what those workers do, hardens the stable parts into plain code, and keeps the result as a versioned model of the company’s data and processes.

First, work with agent workers. Users interact with workers the way they would with Claude, and the workers do the job. Then Logven analyses at night, running dynamic analysis of executions with embeddings, semantic search and similarity algorithms.

Next, it consolidates. Equivalent agents are merged and canonical data models are established. Then it hardens. Approval controls are added at validated decision points, and variable agent handoffs are replaced by generated deterministic code and system integrations.

Finally, it converges. Each worker ends up as a single traceable function. Language models stay only where real ambiguity remains.

Logven is designed to run on customer hardware with smaller models. Today it is built from workers and sheets: the core is the data, sheets are the table view over it, and documents and emails are snapshots of parts of that state.

An example

We explored this approach with a geothermal team in the public sector.

The team manages data from 36 geothermal plants. Its workflow includes extracting values from PDFs, updating spreadsheets and databases, consolidating and analysing data, producing reports, processing responses, and resolving anomalies.

The end state is a dedicated plant-operations system. It emerges from observed work rather than an upfront spec, and it reduces model dependence and inference cost.

What makes it different

The analyst category of “Digital Twin of an Organization” produces digital shadows: models that mirror the company. Logven’s twin runs it, so optimising the model changes the work.

A digital shadow is drawn and maintained by hand, and optimising it yields insight only. Logven’s twin is built from executed work, and optimising it changes how the work is done. Its agents are the means of building the twin, not the product, and the end state is traceable, deterministic software rather than dashboards.

They sell agents. Agents are how we build the twin.

Who it’s for

Logven works with enterprise and medium-to-large companies, for the people who do the work and the people accountable for it.

Technical, highly specialised teams get manual work removed and their expertise captured as software. VPs and department heads get their department as a system they can see, version and optimise.
